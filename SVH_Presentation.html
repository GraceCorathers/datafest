Follow The Journey ↓

Meet Samantha.

She is fighting to stay healthy.

Samantha is a real representation of the data. Like 64.6% of patients surveyed across our healthcare system, she is a single mother, currently battling severe socio-economic stress on top of her physical health conditions.

But her challenge isn't a medical mystery. It's a structural failure in how the health system reaches out to our most vulnerable neighbors.

Caught in the Loop.

Crisis-Driven Care

A statistical archetype identifying patients whose journey entirely lacks continuous outpatient management. Rather than routine check-ups, their only healthcare interaction is severe, repeated Emergency Department visits.

Samantha hasn't seen a primary care provider in over 90 days. But she's not alone. Our data reveals a staggering 46,674 SVH patients trapped in the exact same "Crisis-Driven" loop.

We wait until her condition is severe enough for an ER visit, stabilize her, and release her back into the very environment that triggered the crisis.

It's Bigger Than The Hospital.

SDOH (Social Determinants of Health)

The non-medical factors, such as housing insecurity, lack of transportation, and neighborhood vulnerability, influence 80% of clinical health outcomes.

If we want to fix Samantha's care journey, we cannot just look at hospital waiting times. We must look at the community where she lives, providing mobile care, transportation methods, and active neighborhood engagement.

To understand exactly where we must deploy these community resources, we have plotted the 3-year clinical dataset across the geography of Kansas.

Now, Let's Explore The Data

We've followed Samantha's journey. We've understood the problem. Now it's time to look at the numbers across our entire healthcare system and discover the patterns that emerge when we examine the social determinants of health.

The Takeaway

Our analysis reveals geographic and socioeconomic barriers dictate health outcomes for vulnerable populations long before they reach the emergency room. With thousands locked in a cycle of crisis driven care, SVH must transition from a reactive hospital to a proactive community partner. The solution to this problem is strategically deploying mobile care units to high escalation areas like Geary County, expanding telehealth to reengage isolated patients, and initiating culturally competent outreach. By doing so, SVH can intercept patients in their own neighborhoods, transforming medical crises into sustained, equitable care.
