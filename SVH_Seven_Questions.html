Key findingWhile median gap is 28 days (routine), the P95 is 438 days — a large tail of patients going over a year between visits in the same care journey.
MyChart Status vs. Avg Inter-Visit Gap
FindingGap length is nearly identical across Activated, Pending, and Declined MyChart patients (~110-113d). Non-Standard status patients show notably longer gaps (183d) — likely an administrative artifact.
Race vs. Avg Inter-Visit Gap
FindingAmerican Indian / Alaska Native patients have the shortest avg gap (97d) but this reflects shorter journey durations overall. Asian patients have the longest (122d, 35.9% over 90d). Gaps by race are narrower than escalation differences — condition mix is the stronger driver.

FindingSVI vulnerability shows almost no gradient in gap length (Q1: 31.9% vs Q4: 31.9% over 90d). Social vulnerability does not predict longer inter-visit gaps in this dataset — escalation rate is the more sensitive outcome for SVI.

FindingHigh-repeat conditions are largely clinically appropriate: transplant monitoring, coagulation management, pregnancy, cancer treatment. The genuinely anomalous signal is "Abnormal imaging findings" — repeat imaging for unresolved abnormalities is worth clinical review.
Repeat Test Rate by Visit Type
FindingAnti-coag and treatment visits have very high repeat rates by design (warfarin monitoring, chemotherapy). Initial consult has 1.3% — as expected. Lab Requisition at 65.9% likely reflects duplicate orders vs. completions — a data quality issue, not clinical one.
Repeat Rate vs. Age Decade
FindingInfants (0-9) show high repeat rates (18.7%) due to rapid follow-up for acute conditions. Repeat rates dip in 10-19 (healthiest decade), then rise gradually into older age cohorts. The 90+ group shows the highest rate (14.3%) reflecting complex multi-morbidity.
Repeat Rate by Race & MyChart Status
FindingRepeat test rates are strikingly similar across all racial groups (9.7–12.0%) — no meaningful disparity. MyChart status is also nearly flat (10.6% Activated vs. 14.4% Inactivated). Unspecified-status patients show 25.5% — likely an incomplete-record artifact.

SVI Quartile vs. Escalation Rate
Key findingLiving in a high-vulnerability census tract predicts nearly 2× the escalation rate (6.1% → 11.8%). High-SVI patients also average 2.1× more ED visits per journey. This is the clearest home-environment signal in the data.
SVI Quartile vs. Journey Outcomes
FindingHigh-SVI patients have shorter journeys (fewer total visits, fewer days) — consistent with care drop-out rather than better health. Low-SVI patients show longer, more managed journeys. This suggests structural barriers cause premature journey termination in vulnerable populations.
What we can measure as SDOH proxies: Housing instability → SVI Theme 4 (rpl_t4_housing). No-vehicle households → ep_noveh. Poverty → ep_pov150. Minority/language barriers → SVI Theme 3. Stress screener now available: direct self-reported stress from social_determinants.csv — 54,050 valid responses from 212,357 SDOH patients (25.5% response rate for this specific question).

Distance to Clinic vs. Median Gap (days)
FindingDistance shows a non-linear pattern: the >60 mile group has the highest median gap (48d) as expected, but the 30-60 mile group actually shows the shortest avg gap (104d). The 5-15 mile band (largest group, 213K) drives the system average. Distance alone is a weak predictor of gaps for most patients.
Telemedicine vs. In-Person Visit Gaps
FindingTelemedicine patients have a shorter avg gap (104d vs 119d) but a longer median (37d vs 28d). This suggests telemedicine helps the most engaged long-gap patients reconnect, but doesn't compress routine visit cadence. It may be serving as a bridge for patients who would otherwise have very long gaps.

Single-Visit Escalation Rate by Race
Key findingAmong patients with a single-visit journey (60.9% of all), Black and Native Hawaiian/Pacific Islander patients have 26% escalation rates — more than double White patients (12.1%). These are patients whose first and only SVH encounter is an emergency. This is the clearest racial disparity in the dataset.
County Journey Profiles
FindingGeary County stands out: 24.1% escalation with only 63-day avg journey duration — short, crisis-heavy journeys. Osage and Jefferson show long avg durations (190-200d) with near-zero escalation — well-managed, sustained outpatient care. Douglas has the lowest MyChart adoption (48.4%) despite being an educated university county.

Journey Archetype Distribution
Key finding60.9% of journeys are single-visit — patients who appear once and don't return. Of those, 13.3% arrive in an emergency. Only 9.2% of journeys become Managed Chronic (the ideal care pathway). Crisis Driven and Crisis Chronic combined represent 46,674 journeys — patients whose care consistently involves emergencies.
Avg Journey Duration by Archetype
FindingManaged Chronic journeys last 538 days on average — nearly 1.5 years — reflecting appropriate long-term disease management. Crisis Chronic is notable: 351-day duration with 100% escalation, suggesting patients who are engaged (returning) but never stabilized.
Condition Class — Avg Duration & Escalation
FindingAcute conditions (injury, infection) have short durations (54d) but the highest escalation rate (15.5%) — many acute presentations are severe. Chronic conditions are longer (182d) with lower escalation (9.4%) suggesting better management. "Other" includes screenings and Z-codes.
Archetype × Condition Class
Finding45,049 acute-condition journeys become "Episodic" (337-day avg) — patients with acute issues (fractures, infections) who keep returning, possibly with complications or chronic sequelae. 163K chronic journeys are merely "Episodic" (152d, 2 visits) — a large undertreated chronic disease population.

Key Gaps in Current Dataset
No individual SDOH responsesThe SDOH questionnaire domains are listed but per-patient answers were not released. Food insecurity, utility shutoffs, housing instability, depression scores would directly answer Q3.
No test names within visit typesLab Visit is a container for dozens of different tests. Without CPT or LOINC codes we cannot distinguish "repeat CBC" from "repeat lipid panel."
No ICD-10 for non-primary diagnosesOnly PrimaryDiagnosisKey per encounter. Comorbidity burden (e.g., diabetes + hypertension + CKD) is invisible — a major confounder for escalation rate comparisons across race.
61% missing census block FIPSThe most powerful geographic analyses are limited to 39% of patients. Understanding why FIPS is missing — address quality? rural? — matters for interpreting geographic findings.
